# ADR 0015 — Dataset roles and the spliced identity-theft task

## Status

Accepted. Decisions 1–3 and 7 take effect with this ADR and are documented in
[datasets](../reference/datasets.md). Decisions 4–6 describe the pragmatiq fraud
task. They are implemented in `tfm.data.tasks.identity_theft` and selected
with `data.fraud_labels: spliced_identity_theft`;
`tests/contract/test_identity_theft_splice.py` holds decisions 4 and 5. Evidence:
[`benchmarks/results/pragmatiq_fraud_separability.md`](../../benchmarks/results/pragmatiq_fraud_separability.md).
Amends [ADR 0013](0013-fraud-probe-split-integrity.md) for the pragmatiq
fraud task only; its split rules still hold.

## Context

A transaction foundation model learns one representation of an entity from all
of that entity's channels and reuses it for many tasks. The evidence for that
claim has to come from one population: the same people, their card payments,
transfers, app sessions, trading, and communications, and several downstream
labels on the same histories. Scoring fraud on one dataset and churn on
another would show two models, not one representation reused.

The repository uses three data sources, with different jobs:

- **Generated fixtures** (`tfm.data.synthetic`): tiny, CPU-only, for tests
  and notebooks.
- **IBM TabFormer**: public synthetic card transactions, one channel, one
  task (fraud). The v1 Blueprint was built on it.
- **pragmatiq books**: generated locally with pragmatiq 1.1.0
  ([dynamiq-ai/pragmatiq](https://github.com/dynamiq-ai/pragmatiq)), several
  channels, a profile, a transfer graph, and user-level and event-level labels
  on the same users. Blueprint v2 is built on it.

A separability check on the book the v2 records use
(`synth_100k_gen2_m31_seed0`) found that pragmatiq's own fraud labels are
identified from one transaction. A gradient-boosted classifier on the
transaction's own fields scores 1.000 ROC-AUC: every labelled fraud event is
an online payment abroad, against 3% abroad and 30% online among the labelled
negatives. pragmatiq's generator has no setting that changes this. Its fraud
episodes and the choice of three random negatives per user are fixed in its
source (`episodes.py`, `labels.py`). This repository does not modify pragmatiq.

PersonaLedger
([CapitalOne-Research/PersonaLedger](https://github.com/CapitalOne-Research/PersonaLedger),
[arXiv 2601.03149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03149)) was considered as a
replacement. Its identity-theft task inserts one day of another user's real
transactions into a victim's history, so every fraudulent transaction is an
ordinary one for someone, and only the victim's own history exposes it. Its
data is one channel (card transactions and payments: timestamp, merchant name,
merchant type, card present, amount) with two tasks (illiquidity and identity
theft), so it cannot carry the multi-channel, multi-task evidence either.

## Decision

1. **Each dataset has one role.**
   - Generated fixtures: tests, smoke runs, and CPU notebooks. No result is
     recorded on them.
   - TabFormer: the v1 reproduction and the newcomer teaching series. Card
     fraud only.
   - pragmatiq: the primary Blueprint v2 dataset. Every v2 task (fraud,
     churn, LTV, default, AML) is scored on the same book, the same users, and
     the same pretrained encoder.
   - PersonaLedger: a planned external benchmark for the v2 model on data this
     repository did not generate (its identity-theft and illiquidity tasks).
     Not implemented; see [datasets](../reference/datasets.md) for what must be
     verified first.
2. **Datasets are never mixed.** A recipe config reads one dataset. A
   pretrained checkpoint is evaluated on the dataset it was pretrained on. A
   results record covers one dataset and names it in its header. Numbers from
   different datasets are not pooled, averaged, or put in one table unless each
   row names its dataset and the text says they are not comparable.
3. **pragmatiq is used unmodified.** Books are generated by a released
   pragmatiq version from a world config committed in this repository
   (`configs/data/pragmatiq/world.yaml`). Records name the
   pragmatiq version and the book directory. If a pragmatiq behaviour needs to
   change, the change is proposed upstream.
4. **The pragmatiq fraud task is spliced identity theft.** pragmatiq's own
   `labels/fraud.parquet` is not used as a reported fraud task. The task, built
   from an unmodified book, follows PersonaLedger's method:
   - A **victim** and a **donor** are two users of the book, neither of whom
     has a pragmatiq fraud label. Each user is at most one victim or one donor.
   - A **theft day** is a UTC calendar day inside the victim's active period
     and inside one split's label window (decision 5).
   - The donor's card transactions (`card_payment`, `atm_withdrawal`) and app
     sessions on the theft day are copied into the victim's history with their
     timestamps unchanged. The donor's salary, rent, transfers, and other
     non-card transactions are not copied: a card thief spends on the card.
   - **Labels are event-level.** Copied transactions are fraud (1). The
     victim's own card transactions in the negative window, the
     `negative_window` days before the theft day plus the theft day itself,
     are legitimate (0). Nothing else is labelled.
   - **Donors are matched** by default: the same home country, and a median
     transaction amount within a factor of 1.5 of the victim's. Random donors
     are a documented easier setting: a donor from another country pays
     abroad in another currency, which the profile exposes (0.86 ROC-AUC with
     no history against 0.50 for matched donors).
   - The splice is a deterministic function of the book and a seed, and its
     manifest (victim, donor, theft day, counts) is written beside the split
     manifest.
5. **Leakage rules for the spliced task.**
   - The victim and the donor are in the same entity split, so a donor's
     legitimate transactions are never legitimate on one side and fraud on the
     other.
   - The theft day lies inside the label window of the victim's side, as
     ADR 0013 defines those windows.
   - The pretraining corpus is built from unspliced histories. Spliced events
     exist only in fraud-probe and fraud fine-tuning rows.
   - Forecast labels (`churn_6m`, `ltv_positive`, `default_12m`) are pragmatiq's
     own, computed on unspliced histories, and are scored on unspliced
     histories.
6. **pragmatiq's own fraud labels stay as a smoke label.** Recipes may still
   read them to exercise the fraud path, but records do not report them as a
   fraud result, and any table that shows them marks them as single-event
   identifiable.
7. **Split standard.** Every dataset is split by time into train, validation,
   and test periods; probes are entity-disjoint (ADR 0008, ADR 0013).
   How the cutoffs are chosen depends on the labels:
   - TabFormer has one event-level task and no forecast labels, so the cutoffs
     are v1's: 80% and 90% of cumulative daily transactions
     (`tfm prepare-tabformer`).
   - pragmatiq issues its forecast labels on fixed dates set by the world
     config (2024-07-01 for `default_12m`, 2025-01-01 for `churn_6m` and
     `ltv_positive` in `configs/data/pragmatiq/world.yaml`). The pretraining cutoff must
     precede the earliest of them (ADR 0013, decision 4). The same 80% rule
     would put it at 2025-02-22 and skip every forecast task. The cutoffs are
     therefore chosen from the label calendar and recorded with the share of
     events in each period: `train_end` 2024-06-01 and `validation_end`
     2024-09-01 hold 40%, 15%, and 45% of the book's events.

## Consequences

- The fraud rows of the existing v2 records (`blueprint_v2_hybrid_lab_seeds`,
  `blueprint_v2_hybrid_lab_v1_flat_seeds`, now in `benchmarks/results/archive/`) were scored on pragmatiq's own
  labels. They stay as recorded, marked as single-event identifiable, and are
  superseded by reruns on the spliced task.
- The `evaluation.history_window` and its single-event control apply to the
  spliced task unchanged: one row per labelled transaction, with the history
  rebuilt at that transaction.
- A recipe reading pragmatiq gains a fraud-task choice; the spliced task is
  the default for recorded runs.
- PersonaLedger support needs its own adapter, its own records, and the
  checks listed in [datasets](../reference/datasets.md). It never shares a
  table with pragmatiq results (decision 2).

## Alternatives rejected

- **Patch pragmatiq's generator**, as a fork or at runtime. It is not this
  repository's code; a patched book could not be regenerated from a released
  pragmatiq.
- **Score fraud on TabFormer and the other tasks on pragmatiq.** Different
  people, so no representation is reused across tasks.
- **Replace pragmatiq with PersonaLedger.** One channel and two tasks.
- **Keep pragmatiq's own fraud labels with a disclaimer.** A history-dependence
  comparison cannot be read from a task that one transaction answers.
- **Condition the probe on the single-event signature** (score only online
  payments abroad). The MCC and amount still identify the fraud inside that
  slice, and the slice is a label-derived filter.
